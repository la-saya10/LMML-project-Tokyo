Kaminarimon gate is the entrance gate of Senso-ji, a famous temple in Asakusa, Tokyo.

Kaminarimon ("Thunder Gate") is the outer gate of Senso-ji, Tokyo's oldest Buddhist temple, and one of the city's most photographed landmarks.

Kaminarimon is the gate of Senso-ji and its formal designation is Fūraijin-mon. Fūjin denotes the deity presiding over the wind, whereas Raijin signifies the deity governing thunder.

Kaminarimon gate is the entrance gate to Senso-ji Temple; its formal name is "Fūraijin-mon" (Gate of the Wind and Thunder Gods). The large red lantern hanging in the center stands 3.9 meters tall and weighs approximately 700 kilograms.

Kaminarimon is the main front gate of Senso-ji Temple. It was originally built near Komagata in 942 and was relocated to its current site during the Kamakura period. Upon its relocation, statues of the Wind God (Fujin) and the Thunder God (Raijin) were enshrined within it; this is how it came to be known as the "Furaijin-mon" (Gate of the Wind and Thunder Gods). The gate burned down many times in the past, but it was rebuilt in 1960 thanks to a donation from the founder of Panasonic (then called Matsushita Electric).

The framework of the giant lantern hanging at Kaminarimon is constructed from bamboo harvested in Kyoto's Tanba region, while its body is formed by layering approximately 300 sheets of washi paper over the frame. Since the paper is crafted entirely from Fukui-sourced kozo (mulberry plant), the lantern can truly be described as a 100% Japanese-made creation.
